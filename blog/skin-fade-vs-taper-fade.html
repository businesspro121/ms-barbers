Skin Fade vs Taper Fade: What's the Difference?

By MS Barbers · 1 September 2026 · 3 min read

"Fade" covers a lot of ground, and the two versions clients ask for most often — skin fade and taper fade — get mixed up all the time. Both blend the sides and back from longer to shorter, but how far down they go, and how sharp the finish is, makes them quite different cuts.

What is a skin fade?

A skin fade blends the hair all the way down to bare skin, usually somewhere around the temple and above the ear. It's the boldest version of a fade: high contrast between the longer hair on top and the close-to-nothing at the sides, with a crisp line where the two meet.

What is a taper fade?

A taper fade blends down to a short length rather than all the way to skin. The transition is more gradual, and there's a little more hair left around the hairline and neckline. It's a softer look overall, and one that grows out a bit more gracefully between visits.

The key differences

  • Contrast: skin fades are high-contrast and bold; taper fades are softer and more gradual.
  • Maintenance: skin fades need a trim every 2–3 weeks to stay sharp; tapers can often stretch a little longer.
  • Formality: tapers tend to suit formal or corporate settings; skin fades lean more into statement styles.
  • Hairline: a skin fade removes hair right up to the skin near the temple; a taper leaves it.

Which one should you choose?

If you want maximum contrast and don't mind coming back every couple of weeks, go skin fade. If you'd rather a look that holds up a little longer between cuts and reads a touch more conservative, a taper fade is usually the better call. Both work with a wide range of hair types and top lengths — the fade choice is mostly about how sharp and low-maintenance you want the sides to be.

How to ask your barber for the right one

The clearest way to describe what you want is to name the fade type and where you want it to start — for example "skin fade, blend starting above the ear" or "taper fade, keep some length around the hairline." Bringing a reference photo helps too, since "fade" alone leaves a lot open to interpretation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a skin fade and a taper fade?

A skin fade blends the hair all the way down to bare skin near the hairline, giving high contrast. A taper fade blends down to a short length rather than skin, giving a softer, more gradual transition.

Which is more high-maintenance, a skin fade or a taper fade?

A skin fade needs more frequent visits, typically every 2 to 3 weeks, because the bare-skin blend line grows out and softens quickly. A taper fade holds its shape a little longer.

Is a taper fade more professional than a skin fade?

A taper fade tends to read as slightly more conservative because it keeps some length near the hairline, which is why it's often chosen for formal or corporate settings. A skin fade is bolder and more common for lower, sharper styles.

Can I switch from a taper fade to a skin fade?

Yes, a barber can take a taper down to a skin fade in the same visit. Going the other way, from skin fade back to taper, just takes a few weeks of regrowth before there's enough length to work with.
